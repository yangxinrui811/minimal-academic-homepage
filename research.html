Quantum Mechanics in Water, One Hydrogen at a Time

Chemical intuition treats hydrogen as a classical particle. It is not. I study how quantum tunneling reshapes hydrogen transfer in aqueous and interfacial chemistry — from the racemization of amino acids and drug molecules to proton exchange along water networks — and I build the automated tools that make these computations routine and reproducible.

① Hydrogen-Transfer Tunneling in Water & Biomolecules

Tunneling entered chemistry through a chiral molecule: Hund's 1927 analysis of amino-acid racemization concluded that tunneling is negligible, and the subject slept for nearly a century. My first-author series revisits that verdict with modern electronic-structure and kinetics methods, and finds that water — through its hydrogen-bond network — flips the answer.

  • Amino acid racemization. Quantum tunneling governs a dual-pathway mechanism via water bridges, reconciling pH-dependent experiments — JPCL 2025
  • Thalidomide. Water-bridged proton tunneling accelerates racemization 87–149-fold at body temperature, bringing theory into agreement with experiment — JPCL 2023
  • Water chains. Two co-existing, opposing proton-transfer mechanisms revealed by position along a finite one-dimensional chain — JCP 2024
  • Water clusters. A sequential-flipping mechanism for donor–acceptor exchange in the water trimer — PCCP 2023; quantum resonance tunneling in confined water — Front. Phys. 2022

② Microdroplets & the Air–Water Interface

Micron-sized droplets accelerate reactions by orders of magnitude and sit at the heart of atmospheric aerosol chemistry. As co-first author, I quantified how phenolic compounds self-photopolymerize far faster at the droplet surface than in bulk — a previously hidden pathway feeding atmospheric brown carbon, the sunlight-absorbing component of organic aerosols.

J. Am. Chem. Soc. 148, 31487–31497 (2026) — co-first author

Current directions: reaction kinetics at electrified air–water interfaces, and how interfacial electric fields reshape the barriers of hydrogen-transfer reactions.

③ Methods & Automation

The science above runs on tools I build. AutoTunnel automates the full tunneling-kinetics pipeline — transition-state search, reaction-path reconstruction, barrier-width and asymmetry extraction, tunneling-corrected rate constants — and has supported more than ten publications from our group. A machine-learning framework decodes the kinetic isotope effect–tunneling relationship across reaction families (preprint, open source). For nuclear quantum effects in condensed phases, I couple machine-learning potentials (DeePMD-kit, DP-GEN active learning) with path-integral molecular dynamics.
